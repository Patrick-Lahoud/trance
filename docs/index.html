Being at your desk isn’t the same as being with the work.

Nobody decides to lose an afternoon.

You open a tab. Check something. Watch one video. Come back later.

The minutes are only part of the problem. What disappears is the concentration between them.

Trance turns that invisible gap into something you can see.

What Trance sees

Trance reads activity signals: input recency, cursor movement, the foreground window title. Never the content of your work. Step through the sequence below to see how it responds.

  1. WorkingThe line holds its level and the efficiency keeps counting up.
  2. StillAfter a few seconds without input, the border eases to yellow, a small signal that your attention may have shifted.
  3. DriftingLonger still and it turns red. Trance doesn’t punish the break. It simply shows that your activity changed.
  4. Moving againOne input and the status returns to normal. No lecture, no penalty.

This is activity-aware attention feedback: Trance reacts to signals, never to screen contents, keystrokes, or anything medical. Awareness, not surveillance.

Yours to place

Focus Mode

When you’ve seen enough, the widget compresses to a status pill: the state and the task. Nothing else.

Give the work a name.

Every task gets a color of its own, and the efficiency line changes color at the exact moment the task changes. Color is data here, not decoration.

A break is not failure.

It is a separate part of the session.

Trance tracks breaks separately. Time on a break never counts against your efficiency. The metric simply holds steady while you’re away, then continues where it left off.
